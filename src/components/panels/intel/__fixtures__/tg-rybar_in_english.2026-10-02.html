📝Bankruptcy Conveyor Belt📝

German statistics continue to reliably record records — just not the kind people usually take pride in. In June 2026, courts registered 2,266 company bankruptcy filings — 15.8% more than a year earlier.

For the first half of the year, such filings totaled 12,812, a 6.7% increase compared to the same period in 2025. More bankruptcies occurred only in the first half of 2013 — when 13,253 cases were recorded.

🖍Meanwhile, the sum of creditor claims looks illogical from the outside. Bankruptcies increased by 6.7% (year-on-year), but the sum of claims, conversely, fell — from €28.2 billion a year ago to €18.5 billion now.

The reason is that if major players were going under, debts would be counted in billions per company, and the total sum would only grow along with the number of bankruptcies. But since the sum is falling while the number of cases rises, small and medium-sized businesses are going bankrupt first and foremost.

🔻More statistics details

▪️Transport and warehouse operations are hit hardest — 71.6 bankruptcies per 10,000 companies, followed by hospitality and restaurant business (59.6) and construction (53.4).

▪️Personal bankruptcies are also rising: in June — 6,839 cases (+5.1%), for the half-year — 38,932 (+2.4%).

▪️The list of victims includes quite living stories: an auto industry supplier with 130 years of history, a cardboard factory nearly 150 years old, one of the country's oldest paper mills (289 years in operation), and an auto dealership chain with a thousand employees.


Meanwhile, new companies registered in the half-year outnumber those closed. About 69,600 major startups versus 49,800 closed (overall — 418,100 registrations versus 298,300 closures). In other words: formally the market is not shrinking — the composition of players is simply changing faster than usual.

❗️This looks like a classic picture of economic decline. Old and stable companies with a century-and-a-half history are leaving the market one after another, and new ones quickly take their place — far more vulnerable to the next round of rising costs, credit rates, and weak demand.

Statistics meanwhile record not economic recovery, but its accelerated renewal through bankruptcy.

📝A Parting Gift📝
Japanese left Kyiv with drones

After watching footage of Chinese exercises with unmanned aircraft, the Japanese began to realize that they have nothing to counter flying drones besides traditional air defense systems. Apparently, that's why Tokyo increasingly started turning to the Kyiv regime for help.

A Japanese delegation that arrived in so-called Ukraine this week is not leaving empty-handed. Its head Itsunori Onodera announced that Tokyo will purchase a batch of drones from Ukrainian company SkyFall on a trial basis. He did not specify which unmanned aircraft will be tested in Japan.

Based on the range of issues discussed with representatives of the Kyiv regime, we may be talking about interceptor drones. SkyFall is currently the most hyped Ukrainian company in this field — it develops P1-SUN unmanned aircraft, which were also purchased by the UAE.

🖍There is no talk of a long-term contract yet, but in any case, the SkyFall interceptor drone will become the second Ukrainian development in Japanese service. Recently, Maritime Self-Defense Force acquired supplies from Terra Drone, which owns two Ukrainian firms and produces interceptors.

📌The framework of defense cooperation between the Japanese and Ukrainians continues to take shape, and there is no doubt that Tokyo will soon acquire strike drones of Ukrainian manufacture.

❗️The parties can lay the groundwork for further weapons purchases — it is no coincidence that in spring insiders spoke of the likelihood of concluding a defense agreement. By the way, it will also open the way for supplies of Japanese equipment to so-called Ukraine, and will also facilitate joint production of UAVs — the already created unmanned technology cluster will help with the latter.

• 📝Into the Great Passage📝

Russian forces are pressing the enemy not only near Volchansk. In the Slobozhansky direction, assault troops are also methodically expanding their zone of control in the border region of Kharkiv Region.

Some time ago, reports came in of Russian Armed Forces entering Vysoka Yaruga. Later this was confirmed by footage showing fighters from the "North" grouping raising state flags in various parts of the village.

➡️Now Russian units are developing attacks toward the neighboring Alisovka. Its liberation would allow them to occupy the large forest massifs located in this sector and push the enemy back into the fields on the approaches to Bolshie Prokhody.

❗️Nevertheless, one should hardly expect an assault on the settlement itself. Ukrainian formations previously set up several defensive lines in its area, an assault on which would only make sense in the event of a large-scale offensive operation aimed at advancing toward Kharkiv.
